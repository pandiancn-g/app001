Cost of Living Impact Analyzer

An AI-powered Bayaan module for SCAD Research & Innovation economists to integrate prices, wages, housing, food, fuel, and household survey data into detailed cost-of-living analysis for Abu Dhabi.

Help economists measure, compare, and simulate how rising living costs affect different population groups in Abu Dhabi.

Instead of only showing headline CPI, the tool explains the real pressure felt by each segment.

1. Objective details

What the objective means inside the application.

Goal: Equip R&I economics specialists with an AI analytical tool inside Bayaan that combines CPI, wage, housing, food, fuel, and household survey data to produce distributional cost-of-living analysis by income group, nationality, and family composition, with policy simulation for rent caps, subsidies, and price changes.
A

Integrate economic datasets

The tool connects multiple sources that economists currently combine manually.

CPI categories Wages Housing rents Food prices Fuel prices Household survey
B

Analyze specific groups

Economists choose the population segment and the AI calculates that group's effective inflation and purchasing-power change.

Income group Nationality Family composition Household size Housing tenure
C

Simulate policy options

Users can test hypothetical policy changes before decisions are made and see which groups benefit or face extra pressure.

Rent caps Subsidy changes Fuel adjustment Food shocks School fees

2. How the AI workflow works

From raw data to an economist-ready answer.

Step 1

Ingest data

Pull CPI, wages, rents, prices, tariffs, and HIES expenditure shares.

Step 2

Build segment

Filter by income, nationality, household type, and geography.

Step 3

Re-weight basket

Use the segment's spending pattern instead of the average CPI basket.

Step 4

Calculate impact

Compare effective inflation, wages, real income, and category pressure.

Step 5

Explain and export

Generate charts, insight text, methodology notes, and files.

5. Datasets and fields needed

The analyzer needs these datasets to create reliable results.

Dataset Why needed Key fields
CPI by COICOP category Measures price change by category. Period, COICOP code, category name, CPI value, inflation rate, geography, CPI weight, base year
Household expenditure survey Shows how each household group spends money. Household ID, income group, nationality, household size, children, rent, food, transport, utilities, education, survey weight
Wage and income statistics Compares income growth against cost growth. Period, wage, household income, sector, occupation, age group, gender, nationality, income decile, region
Housing rent data Calculates rent burden and rent cap impact. Period, location, property type, bedrooms, rent value, rent index, tenancy type
Food and fuel prices Measures pressure from essential goods and transport costs. Product, unit, price, fuel type, fuel price per liter, location, period
Utility and education fees Supports tariff and school fee simulations. Utility type, tariff band, customer type, subsidy amount, school type, grade level, annual tuition
Demographic register Defines population segments. Person ID, household ID, age, gender, nationality, household composition, region, student indicator

Sample insight summary

  • Headline CPI is 2.0%, but the selected household group experiences 3.8% effective inflation because housing and food have higher weights in their budget.
  • Wage growth of 2.4% does not fully offset price increases, creating a real income decline of 1.4%.
  • The strongest pressure comes from rent, followed by food and education costs.
  • A 3% rent cap could reduce effective inflation by 0.7 percentage points for affected renter families.
